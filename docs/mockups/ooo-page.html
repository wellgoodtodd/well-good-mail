Out of Office: three ways to lay out the page

Everything it does today stays. What changes is the order, the size of things, and how it reads. All three share the same new pieces, shown at the bottom: accounts as switches, a clearer name for "Own message", and templates that notice when you've changed them.

Each section is one card of rows, label on the left and the control on the right, the same as General or Notifications. The cards are all the same width, so the eye just goes down the page. "Who gets it" becomes a drop-down instead of three radio buttons with long notes. The sentence at the bottom reads back the whole plan before you press the button. "Suggest Out of Office from my calendar" moves to Settings › Calendar & Reminders, since it's about the calendar, not this time away.

The most compact, and it's obvious what people will receive. Click an account in From to turn it on or off. The catch: the From chips don't show each account's state (On until Friday) as clearly as switches do, and a different message per account is harder to fit in.

Shared by all three: "Own message", renamed

You've got it right: one message goes to every ticked account, unless you give an account its own, for example a professional one for Well Good Media and a casual one for your personal Gmail. The button on each account goes. Instead there's one switch on the Message card:

todd@wellgood.mediatbweller@gmail.com

Off: one message for all. On: a tab for each account that's switched on, each starting as a copy of the shared message.

Things to decide
1. Layout A, B or C?
2. Is "Different message for each account" clear enough?
3. Should the "Update Away day test too?" question come up when you press Schedule, or is the Changed label with its two buttons enough on its own?
